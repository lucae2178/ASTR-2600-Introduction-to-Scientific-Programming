1 Physics Background: Exoplanets, Known and Unknown¶

The fraction of star light that a planet blocks, often known as the transit depth 𝐷, is the ratio of cross-sectional areas, given by the following equation

D = $$\frac{np.pi*R^{2}<sub>planet</sub>}{np.pi*R^{2}<sub>star</sub>}$$ = $$(\frac{np.pi*R<sub>planet</sub>}{np.pi*R<sub>star</sub>})^{2}$$

A typical Jupiter-sized planet transiting a Sun-like star has a transit depth of 0.01, meaning it blocks 1% of the star’s light.

The typical flux of a planet at a distance "a" away from a star is given by

Fplanet = $$\frac{L<sub>star</sub>}{4*np.pi*a^{2}}$$

1 what's in the files¶

The file, exoplanet.csv, is a .csv file, so I will be using pandas to read it. The file also has a list of rows. Each row has 4 columns for each exoplanet. The first column is the exoplanet's name. The second column is the exoplanets' radii in Earth radii. The third column is the star's radius in solar radii. The third column is the energy flux the planet receives from its star relative to what Earth receives from the sun. exoplanets.csv has 2415 rows, and rows 1-7 ar headers

I will make a data table with the index number of the planet in the furthest left column, then the name of the planet in the next left column, then the other three data points in succeeding columns.
